Example: \(f(\mathbf x)=\tfrac12(x_1^2+2x_2^2)\), \(\mathbf x^\star=0\). Move the slider to see an overshoot.

One gradient step

\(D_t=\|\mathbf x_t-\mathbf x^\star\|_2^2\), \(e_t=f(\mathbf x_t)-f^\star\).

\[\begin{aligned}D_{t+1}&=\|\mathbf x_t-\mathbf x^\star-\eta\mathbf g_t\|_2^2\\&=D_t-2\eta\mathbf g_t^\top(\mathbf x_t-\mathbf x^\star)+\eta^2\|\mathbf g_t\|_2^2.\end{aligned}\]

Use \(\|\mathbf a-\mathbf b\|^2=\|\mathbf a\|^2-2\mathbf a^\top\mathbf b+\|\mathbf b\|^2\),
with \(\mathbf a=\mathbf x_t-\mathbf x^\star\) and \(\mathbf b=\eta\mathbf g_t\).

First-order condition for convexity:

\[f(\mathbf x^\star)\ge f(\mathbf x_t)+\mathbf g_t^\top(\mathbf x^\star-\mathbf x_t).\]\[\begin{aligned}2\eta e_t&\le2\eta\mathbf g_t^\top(\mathbf x_t-\mathbf x^\star)\\&=D_t-D_{t+1}+\eta^2\|\mathbf g_t\|_2^2.\end{aligned}\]

Internal distances cancel. Only the endpoints remain.

Sum the one-step bound

\[2\eta\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}e_t\le\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(D_t-D_{t+1})+\eta^2\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\|\mathbf g_t\|_2^2.\]

The accumulated distance terms are exactly

\[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(D_t-D_{t+1})=D_0-D_T\le R^2.\]

\(D_t=\|\mathbf x_t-\mathbf x^\star\|_2^2\), \(R^2=D_0\). We bound a sum of errors using one initial distance.

Curves show the theoretical upper bound, normalized by \(RG/\sqrt T\). They are not measured convergence trajectories.

Choose the step size

Assume a gradient-norm bound \(G\): \(\|\mathbf g_t\|_2\le G\), with \(R,G>0\).

\[\frac1T\sum_t e_t\le\frac{R^2}{2\eta T}+\frac{\eta G^2}{2}.\]

Distance term: a step that is too small makes this large.
Gradient term: a step that is too large makes this large.

\[\eta_\star=\frac{R}{G\sqrt T},\qquad f(\overline{\mathbf x}_T)-f^\star\le\frac{RG}{\sqrt T}.\]

To obtain \(f(\overline{\mathbf x}_T)-f^\star\le\varepsilon\), choose \(T\ge(RG/\varepsilon)^2\): \(O(\varepsilon^{-2})\) iterations for fixed \(R,G\).

\(\overline{\mathbf x}_T=T^{-1}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbf x_t\). Convexity justifies the averaged output.
